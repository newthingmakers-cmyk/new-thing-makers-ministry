01 — Overview

What is the Gospel of Mark?

The Gospel of Mark presents Jesus Christ through His ministry, teachings, suffering, death, and resurrection.

Key Scripture: Mark 10:45

The Son of Man came not to be served but to serve.

02 — Who Was Mark?

John Mark: His Name and Family

The New Testament refers to him as John, also called Mark (Acts 12:12, 25; 15:37). John was a Jewish name, while Mark was a Roman name used in the wider Greco-Roman world. The Bible does not explain why he used both names.

Acts 12:12 tells us that his mother was Mary and that believers gathered at her house to pray. When Peter was released from prison, he went to this house, where many believers had assembled. This shows that Mark's family was connected with the early Jerusalem church. The passage does not tell us Mark's age or formal role at that time.

Mark's Ministry with Barnabas and Paul

Acts 12:25 says that John Mark went with Barnabas and Saul (Paul). Later, Mark accompanied them on part of their first missionary journey and served as a helper (Acts 13:5). At Perga, however, he left them and returned to Jerusalem (Acts 13:13). The text does not state his reason, so we should avoid claiming to know why he left.

When Paul and Barnabas planned another journey, they disagreed about taking Mark with them. Barnabas wanted to take him, but Paul did not think it wise because Mark had left them earlier. They separated: Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus, while Paul chose Silas (Acts 15:36–40). This was a serious disagreement, but it was not the end of Mark's story.

Mark's Later Service

Later New Testament letters list Mark among Paul's fellow workers (Colossians 4:10; Philemon 24). In 2 Timothy 4:11, Paul asks Timothy to bring Mark, describing him as useful for the ministry. These passages show that Mark later became a valued coworker. The Bible does not explain every step in how their relationship changed.

Mark and Peter

In 1 Peter 5:13, Peter refers to Mark as 'my son,' a phrase commonly understood as expressing a close spiritual relationship rather than biological parenthood. Early Christian tradition connects Mark's Gospel with Peter's preaching and testimony. This is an important historical tradition, but it is not stated directly inside the Gospel itself.

Authorship, Date, Audience, and Purpose

The Gospel itself does not name its author. Early Christian tradition identifies the author with John Mark. Many scholars date the Gospel around AD 65–75, though the exact date is debated. Its original audience is also debated; some scholars suggest a Roman or broader Gentile audience. These are scholarly proposals, not details explicitly stated in the Gospel.

Mark presents Jesus' identity, authority, teaching, compassion, suffering, death, and resurrection. Mark 10:45 summarizes Jesus' servant mission: He came not to be served but to serve and to give His life as a ransom for many. The Gospel also invites readers to consider the meaning and cost of following Jesus.

03 — Purpose and Main Themes

What should we look for?

  • The identity and authority of Jesus Christ.
  • Jesus' teaching, compassion, and mighty works.
  • The meaning and cost of following Jesus.
  • The suffering, death, and resurrection of Jesus.

04 — How to Study

A simple study approach

  1. Read the passage carefully.
  2. Observe what the passage says.
  3. Understand its meaning in context.
  4. Consider what it teaches about Jesus Christ.
  5. Apply the teaching faithfully in daily life.
